Compressing Arabic Prompts Without Losing the Message

Badr Abouabdou  · Dissertation Advisor: Laud Charles Ochei
University of Liverpool
MSc Artificial Intelligence — online programme

1Why Arabic prompts need compression

Commercial LLMs charge per token — and their tokenisers, optimised for English, fragment Arabic words into far more tokens than English words of equal meaning. Measured on this project's benchmark, Arabic costs ≈ 4.4 tokens per word versus ≈ 1.3 for English: Arabic users pay roughly three times more per word.

Prompt compression removes low-value tokens before the prompt is sent — but every published method was built and tested on English. This project asks whether those methods transfer to Arabic, and builds APCS: a selector that recommends the right compression strategy for each Arabic prompt.

2Benchmark & method

AraPromptBench — 1,000 Modern Standard Arabic prompts: 850 sourced from six public corpora (CIDAR, XL-Sum, EASC, TyDi-QA, ARCD, Aya) with per-prompt licence tracking, plus 150 synthetic creative briefs; 80/20 development/test split with a contamination-free test set.

  • Methods compared: LLMLingua (perplexity-based), LLMLingua-2 (classifier-based), random deletion, no compression — at keep-rates 0.7 / 0.5 / 0.3.
  • Two-level evaluation: token savings (TCR) and semantic fidelity as BERTScore-F1 — between prompt texts, and between the LLM's answers to compressed vs original prompts.
  • Statistics: paired Wilcoxon, McNemar, seed-42 reproducibility manifests.

3English defaults break on Arabic

LLMLingua's default GPT-2 scorer reads Arabic as raw bytes — each letter is two bytes — so deleting "tokens" slices letters in half:

زيادة  →  �يادة

Character corruption in 38 of 45 outputs; crashes on all long prompts; 45% of output words broken. Substituting a multilingual scorer (Qwen2.5-0.5B) fixed corruption — but revealed that LLMLingua-1 refuses to compress short prompts at all (96–98% of QA/creative prompts returned untouched). LLMLingua-2's multilingual classifier needed no modification — hitting every compression target on every category.

4Measure answers, not prompts — the ranking flips

Judged by prompt-text similarity, random deletion looks better than intelligent compression. Judged by whether the LLM's answer is preserved, the ranking inverts — both with p < 10⁻¹¹. Studies using the cheap metric would crown the wrong method.

5APCS — the right strategy per prompt

No single choice is optimal for even a third of prompts. APCS extracts lightweight features and applies an empirically calibrated, fully explainable rule:

if token_count < 120 → do not compress
otherwise → LLMLingua-2 @ keep-rate 0.5

APCS beats every fixed baseline (McNemar exact, p ≤ 6×10⁻³) and captures 80% of the theoretical-maximum token savings at near-optimal fidelity — using one explainable rule. Total API cost of all experiments: under $2.

6Conclusions & next steps

  • English-default compression does not transfer to Arabic; multilingual classifier-based compression (LLMLingua-2) does.
  • Prompt length — not morphology — drives compression tolerance; QA prompts suffer most once the LLM's noise ceiling is accounted for.
  • Adaptive selection beats any fixed strategy: the premise of APCS holds for Arabic.
  • Next: final held-out evaluation on the 1,000-prompt benchmark with a current-generation LLM, and public release of AraPromptBench and the APCS package.
